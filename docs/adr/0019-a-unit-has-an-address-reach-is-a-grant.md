# ADR 0019: A unit has an address; reach is a grant the owner makes and the platform enforces

**Status:** Draft · 2026-11-10
**Supersedes / relates to:** docs/stories.md (networking as sockets, the architecture-changing row); open finding L4.6 (`expose` depends on undesigned networking); ADR 0001 (a pipe is a staged artifact; docs/debate-review.md, "sockets only for services"); ADR 0005 (the session is a server-side pod); ADR 0010 (platform defaults, packages; rule L7.6, hostname egress); ADR 0011 (mounts and their options, `caps` per device, residency in amendment 3, `/sys/k8s/<id>/link` in rule L10.5); ADR 0013 decision 7 (the `expose` entry, marked `open`) and decision 3 (`explain`); ADR 0016 decisions 3 and 8 (group-owned objects, a namespace belongs to one group); ADR 0017 decision 9 (`expose --public` is a gate); ADR 0018 (labels signal, admission decides; package mappings in `caps`; the shell-only mode of rule L19.4)

## Context

Every other abstraction in the design moves data as artifacts between stages, and ADR 0001 keeps it that way: no socket joins two stages of a pipeline. What is left is the long-running process that others call: an API, a notebook, a model server, a consumer's health port. The stories need four things of it and the design gives none of them a home. US-04 runs `expose notebook --internal` so a person's browser reaches a notebook, and `expose churn-api` so other services reach an API. Walkthrough 2 shifts route weights with `release --canary`. Walkthrough 9 runs an agent whose package declares the hosts it may call. Walkthrough 7 creates a tenant namespace with a default-deny policy. ADR 0013 fixed the synopsis of `expose` and marked its behaviour `open`; this record closes it.

Facts that constrain the design:

- Inside a cluster a Service gives a set of pods one stable name, `<service>.<namespace>.svc`, resolved by CoreDNS; EndpointSlices say which pods are ready behind it. Nothing in the API names a socket by path.
- The Gateway API splits roles: the platform owns a Gateway (listeners, addresses, certificates, which namespaces may attach), an application team attaches HTTPRoute, GRPCRoute or, on the experimental channel, TCPRoute objects to it. Ingress predates the split and carries controller-specific annotations.
- NetworkPolicy is namespaced, additive, L3/L4 and selects by pod and namespace labels. It has no host names, no identities and no deny rule; a default-deny is an empty-selector policy that nothing else then widens unless someone writes an allow. Whether it is enforced at all depends on the CNI. AdminNetworkPolicy and BaselineAdminNetworkPolicy are cluster-scoped and let the platform set rules a namespace cannot override, where the CNI implements them.
- Host-name egress needs an FQDN-aware CNI or an egress proxy (rule L7.6).
- Reaching a pod from outside the cluster network without any exposure is possible only through the API server's `pods/portforward` subresource, which RBAC authorises per identity. It is TCP, one pod at a time, and slow by data-plane standards.
- Cross-cluster service discovery exists in several forms (the Multi-Cluster Services API with `clusterset.local`, Cilium ClusterMesh, Submariner, mesh federation, cloud multi-cluster gateways), and no fleet can be assumed to have any of them.
- A public address costs money (the gateway's share, data transfer out per gigabyte), needs a certificate and a DNS record, and is the one place the internet meets the fleet.

## Decision

1. **A socket belongs to a unit; a pipe is never a socket.** Only a unit (`run --unit`, ADR 0013 decision 7) has an address. A Job, a batch index and a pipeline stage do not, and `expose` on a pid is refused with the `run --unit` spelling. Stages exchange artifacts (ADR 0001) on every backend and across every cluster; the planner never inserts a socket, a stream or a mesh hop between stages. A unit declares its ports in its executable's annotation or with `run --port=<n>[/<proto>]`; `expose` without a port uses the declared one and refuses when there are several.

2. **Reach is one ordered axis, span is another.** `expose <unit> [<port>] [--cluster|--internal|--public] [--global] [--allow=<group>…] [--off]` (this revises the synopsis in ADR 0013 decision 7).

   | Reach | Who can connect | What the planner writes | Gate |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `--cluster` (default) | pods in the owning group's namespaces on that cluster, and pods of groups named by `--allow` | a Service, and the ingress allow in the unit's NetworkPolicy | none |
   | `--internal` | people in the allowed groups, from the organisation's network, through the platform's internal gateway behind the login (ADR 0016) | the above, an HTTPRoute or GRPCRoute on the platform's internal Gateway, and the auth policy the gateway package maps (`caps`) | none |
   | `--public` | anyone on the internet | the above on the platform's public Gateway, a hostname, a certificate and a DNS record through the packages that provide them | ADR 0017 approval, always |

   `--global` spans every cluster the unit is released to (ADR 0013, `release --to`) and requires a span capability on every one of them: `caps: mcs` for `--cluster` and `--internal`, `caps: global-gateway` for `--public`. A unit whose residency (ADR 0011 amendment 3) excludes a cluster is not spanned to it, and `explain` prints the clusters left out and why. Re-running `expose` with a lower reach narrows it and deletes the routes the higher one wrote; `--off` removes the Service and every route. The unit keeps running.

3. **The owner grants reach; the caller declares what it reaches.** Under ADR 0016 a namespace belongs to one group and that group is the tenant. Pods in the owning group's namespaces reach each other; nothing reaches across groups unless both sides say so. The owner of the target grants with `expose <unit> --allow=<group>` (any member of the owning group may, as with any group-owned object); the caller declares with `run --connect=<path>` or `batch --connect=<path>`, where the path is the unit's path under a mount (`/clusters/prod-east-1/risk/scorer`). The planner resolves the path to the Service name, injects it as `CONVOY_ADDR_<UNIT>` into the caller, writes the caller's egress allow, and refuses at plan time when the target has not granted the caller's group, naming the target's group and the `expose --allow` spelling. A grant is a receipted act on the unit, revocable with `expose <unit> --allow=-<group>`.

4. **Egress is declared by the executable and capped by the mount.** An executable's annotation lists the hosts it calls (`egress:`, as in walkthrough 9); a mount carries `-o egress=none|declared|<host list>`, the platform's default for the mount profile (ADR 0010) and visible in `mount`. The planner emits the intersection: in-cluster targets as NetworkPolicy, host names through `caps: fqdn-policy` or the platform's egress proxy (rule L7.6), never by resolving names to addresses at plan time. A command whose declared egress the mount forbids is refused before anything is written, naming the host and the option. DNS, the artifact store of the mount and the identity provider are always reachable; `explain` lists them as such.

5. **The platform owns the boundary, the shell writes inside it.** Gateways, their addresses, their certificate issuers, the public DNS zones, WAF and rate-limit policy, and the default-deny for every group namespace belong to the platform and ship with the namespace template (ADR 0010, walkthrough 7). Where the CNI implements it (`caps: admin-network-policy`), the default-deny and the group boundary are a BaselineAdminNetworkPolicy, so a namespace policy written outside the shell cannot widen reach across groups; elsewhere they are a namespace NetworkPolicy and `caps` reports `netpol: namespace-only`. The shell writes only Services, routes that attach to a platform Gateway, and NetworkPolicy objects labelled with the `convoy.io/` set (ADR 0018); it never creates a Gateway, a LoadBalancer Service, a NodePort or a pod on the host network, and a unit that asks for one is refused.

6. **An unenforced boundary is reported, never assumed.** A cluster whose CNI does not enforce NetworkPolicy reports `netpol: none`. There, `explain` prints `reachability not enforced` on every plan that writes, `--allow` grants are recorded but marked advisory, `--public` is refused, and a mount with `egress=` other than its platform default refuses to run there, because the option cannot be met. Under `netpol: namespace-only`, `explain` prints the mode, as rule L19.4 does for admission.

7. **The session reaches units through the API server, as the user.** A command in the session (`curl`, `psql`, a debugger) resolves unit names through the session's `/etc/hosts` view: each unit and each running index (`pid-7610-3`) in the session's mounted namespaces maps to a loopback address backed by a port-forward opened on first connect under the user's own token (ADR 0016), so RBAC on `pods/portforward` decides, never a network policy keyed on the session pod. It reaches one ready pod, says which, and is meant for looking, not for data; `explain` prints that a command in the session is port-forwarded and that a data path belongs in a stage. The thin client can carry the same forward to the user's machine, `attach -L <local>:<unit>:<port>`, receipted per open. The session pod's own egress is the platform's session policy (DNS, the API servers and stores of mounted devices, the identity provider); a local command that declares more is promoted to a Job, where decision 4 applies.

8. **Names follow ownership.** Inside a cluster a unit is `<unit>` in its own namespace and `<unit>.<namespace>` from another, as CoreDNS already answers; the shell never runs its own resolver for workloads. `--internal` and `--public` hostnames default to `<unit>.<group slug>.<platform domain>` with the slug of ADR 0018 decision 7; `--host=<name>` is accepted only under a domain the inventory delegates to the owning group, and a name in use is refused with its owner. A certificate is never optional on `--internal` or `--public`.

9. **Addresses are files, `netstat` is their table.** `/sys/unit/<name>/net/` holds `addr` (one line per reach and span), `ports`, `allow`, `routes` (with weights, which `release --canary` writes; ADR 0013), `endpoints` (ready and total), `cert` (issuer and expiry) and `policy` (the policy in force, with the enforcement mode of decision 6). `netstat` lists, for the cwd's mount or every mount with `-A`, each unit with its address, reach, span, allowed groups, endpoints and certificate expiry. It shows sockets as the platform declares them; live connections and flows only under `caps: flows` (Hubble, VPC flow logs), and `unknown` otherwise, never empty.

10. **Exposure is priced and receipted.** `explain expose` prints the gateway's hourly share, the price per gigabyte out for the reach and the span from `/sys/k8s/<id>/link` and the provider's list (rule L10.5), the certificate and DNS objects to be written, and who will be able to connect. Every change of reach, span or grant is a lifecycle event in the unit's receipt (ADR 0013); traffic cost settles into it as an interval where the gateway package or `caps: flows` reports bytes.

11. **A mesh is a package, and refines without replacing.** Where a service mesh is installed and declares its mapping in `caps` (ADR 0018 decision 6), `--allow` also compiles to the mesh's identity-aware policy (for example an Istio AuthorizationPolicy with the group's service identity, ADR 0016) and `--global` may use mesh federation as its span capability. The NetworkPolicy of decision 3 is written either way, so removing the mesh never opens anything.

## Declined

| Proposal | Why not | What replaces it |
|---|---|---|
| sockets or streams between pipeline stages, across clusters or within one | no backpressure contract across pods, no replay, no cache; ADR 0001 already chose | staged artifacts; a socket only on a unit |
| `expose pid 8080` on a Job or a batch index | a process that ends has no address worth publishing | `run --unit`; `pid-<pid>-<index>` in the session's hosts view for a look |
| a LoadBalancer Service per exposed unit | one cloud load balancer and address per unit: cost, address sprawl, and the internet boundary spread across teams | routes on the platform's Gateways (decision 5) |
| Ingress as the default resource | controller-specific annotations, no role split between platform and team | Gateway API routes; a cluster with only an Ingress controller gets routes through a package mapping, or a refusal naming the capability |
| a network policy keyed on the session pod for interactive access | the session pod carries one user's many groups, which a fixed label set cannot express (ADR 0018), and a pod selector is not an identity | port-forward under the user's token (decision 7) |
| resolving host names to addresses at plan time for egress | addresses change under the policy; CDNs rotate them hourly | `caps: fqdn-policy` or the egress proxy (rule L7.6) |
| the shell installing a mesh, a CNI or a DNS server | platform components are the platform's (ADR 0010) | `caps` reports what exists; packages provide the rest |
| reach granted by the caller alone (`--connect` opens the target) | the target's owner would never have said yes | both sides: `--allow` by the owner, `--connect` by the caller (decision 3) |
| `--public` without a certificate for internal tools | a plain-text public listener is the incident this design should make impossible | certificates always (decision 8) |

## Consequences

- L4.6 is drafted: `expose` has a meaning per reach and span, an owner's grant, a caller's declaration, a price and a receipt, and US-04's `expose notebook --internal` and `expose churn-api` each compile to named objects.
- ADR 0013 decision 7 changes: the `expose` synopsis gains `--cluster`, `--off` and `--allow`, `--global` becomes a span orthogonal to reach, and `run` and `batch` gain `--connect=<path>`. `attach -L` is a client option, not a verb.
- New `caps` keys, each reported per device (rule L10.9): `gateway` (internal and public, with the classes of route each listener admits), `gateway-tcp`, `netpol` (`enforced`, `namespace-only`, `none`), `admin-network-policy`, `mcs`, `global-gateway`, `certs`, `dns`, `flows`, and a mesh's mapping; `fqdn-policy` exists (rule L7.6).
- Cost: M11 carries the verbs, the session hosts view and `attach -L`, the `/sys/unit/<name>/net/` files and `netstat`; M7 carries the namespace template's default-deny and the group boundary; M8 the delegated domains in the inventory and the span capabilities; M9 the package mappings for gateways, certificates, DNS and meshes.
- The design now has one place where the internet meets the fleet, owned by the platform and gated by ADR 0017; every exposure inside it is visible from a file and a table.
- Open, to be settled against this ADR: UDP and other non-HTTP public listeners beyond `gateway-tcp`; the exact auth policy for `--internal` when the gateway package offers none; long-lived connections (WebSockets, gRPC streams) across a `release` that shifts weights; IPv6-only clusters; whether `--allow` should accept a single user as well as a group (ADR 0016 says rights go to groups, which argues no); the session hosts view when two mounts hold units of the same name.
- [US-20](../user-stories/US-20-expose-and-reach.md) probes this record under the SRE, DevOps and Security personas with a cross-team grant, a gated public address, a span refused for capability and residency, a notebook behind the login, a replica debugged from the session and the laptop, a cluster whose CNI enforces nothing and a canary under open streams. It raised eleven findings; two propose revisions to this draft, not yet applied: L20.2 (session reach is a port-forward only for units the user's groups own, and each replica is addressable; a granted group reaches through the internal route; decision 7) and L20.8 (TLS termination and access logs are residency facts; decision 2). Eight are rules: intent on managed units (L20.1), hostnames held after withdrawal (L20.3), private issuers for internal names (L20.4), cross-zone and cross-region pricing (L20.5), revocation that lists and tells callers (L20.6), route conflict pre-flight (L20.7), promotion re-planning reach (L20.9) and open streams with a drain window across a release (L20.10). L20.11 accepts that an internal notebook is the group's.
