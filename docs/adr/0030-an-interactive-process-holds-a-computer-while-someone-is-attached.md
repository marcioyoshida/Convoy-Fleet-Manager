# ADR 0030: An interactive process holds a computer while someone is attached

**Status:** Draft · 2026-11-14
**Supersedes / relates to:** open finding L34.1 (interactive work has no process class, attach path, idle rule, end or lineage); ADR 0025 decisions 2, 3, 4, 8 and 9 and its open item on interactive work on a scheduler (the computer contract and its `stage` field, the kinds, placement, external operators, allocations); ADR 0029's open item on interactive sessions over staged data; ADR 0020 decisions 4, 5, 6 and 7 (the token, the attach channel, a shell per terminal, the session never ends a remote process); ADR 0019 decisions 2, 3 and 7 (reach, grants, the gateway and `attach -L`); ADR 0016 decisions 4, 5, 7 and 11 (the exchange per open, the group's service identity, grants by group, the session's residency); ADR 0004 amendment 3 (the inherited contract); ADR 0001 amendment 1 (non-deterministic commands); ADR 0002 (executable annotations); ADR 0007 amendment 5 (the scrub); ADR 0023 (telemetry the shell reads); ADR 0027 decisions 3 and 4 (events, groups and subscribers); ADR 0028 decision 8 (domain pools); ADR 0029 decision 4 (pinned inputs); ADR 0010 rule L4.3 (idle units); accepted limitations L20.11, L21.9 and L24.11; US-04's notebook unit; the command map rows for `fg`, `bg`, `tty` and `nice`

## Context

Convoy has two kinds of remote process. A stage converges: it runs to an exit code under a contract (ADR 0004). A unit serves: it answers requests until it is stopped (ADR 0013, ADR 0019). A person at a terminal or a notebook is neither. The work holds a computer's resources, often its scarcest (a GPU, an allocation on a cluster someone else runs), while someone thinks; it is idle most of the time by any measure a service would use, and busy by none; its state is in a kernel's memory and in the person's head; and it should end when the person has gone, not when a request stops arriving or a deadline passes.

The design never gave that work a shape. US-04 made a notebook a unit with one GPU, idled by the unit rule of L4.3; the debate review made a shell with a terminal a bare pod; on Slurm, VM pools, functions and batch services there was nothing, and ADR 0025 and ADR 0029 left it open. US-34 spent a week in that gap: a notebook holding a whole B200 inside an NVLink domain at 3 % for three days because the browser tab counted as traffic, a Jupyter token pasted into chat, restricted rows saved in a notebook and pushed outside the residency, a sweep launched from a cell as the group's service identity with no person and no budget, a kernel lost to a drain with nobody told, Jupyter under `batch` to get eight GPUs, `salloc` from a university login node outside Convoy, `^Z` that kept an allocation counting, a closed laptop and an 8-hour limit with no warning, and the platform's JupyterHub as a second spawner with a second idle rule.

Facts that constrain the design:

- Interactive applications come in two forms: a terminal, which the session's attach channel already carries (ADR 0020 decision 5), and a browser application (Jupyter, an editor server), which needs an address, a login and a proxy (ADR 0019 decision 7).
- Schedulers already have an interactive path: `salloc` and `srun --pty` on Slurm, an instance from a pool, a pod with a terminal; functions and batch services have none.
- Notebook servers authenticate with their own tokens and are written to be reached by whoever holds the token.
- A kernel's memory cannot in general be checkpointed and restored across a node, a driver or a GPU.
- Platforms often already run a notebook hub with its own spawner, profiles and idle culler.

## Decision

1. **Interactive is a process class, beside stages and units.** A process is a stage, a unit or interactive. An interactive process has a pid, `/proc/<pid>` and `/sys/proc/<pid>/` like any other, a budget and a deadline from the contract, and no floor or retries; its exit is its shell's or application's exit. The computer contract's `stage` field (ADR 0025 decision 2) gains an `interactive` shape per kind: on Kubernetes a pod with a terminal or a browser application, or the platform's notebook hub (decision 10); on Slurm an `salloc` allocation and an `srun --pty` step; on a VM pool an instance; on functions and batch services `none`, and `explain` refuses there with exit 3.

2. **`run -it` opens a terminal, and `run -it --app=<executable>` a browser application.** `run -it [--gpu=<share>] [--on <computer>] [--wait=<duration>] [--keep] [<command>]` starts an interactive process whose terminal is the foreground job of the shell that started it, with `fg`, `bg` and `jobs` as the command map gives them. `--app=<executable>` starts an executable annotated `interactive` (ADR 0002: `jupyter`, `code-server`, a package's own) and gives it an address on the gateway for its owner. An executable annotated `interactive` is refused under `batch` with exit 3 and the `run -it` spelling. A notebook is no longer a unit; US-04's `run --unit notebook jupyter` reads as `run -it --app=jupyter`.

3. **It is reached only through the session and the gateway, by the people it names.** A terminal is carried by the session's attach channel; a browser application is reached through the platform's gateway at a path for the process, with the login of ADR 0016, never by a token of the application's own, which Convoy keeps and never hands out. The owner attaches; another person attaches only under the owner's grant (`attach <pid> --grant=<user|group>[:write]`), read-only unless the grant says `write`; `who` and the receipt list everyone attached and when. There is no port-forward to another person's process and no `exec` into its pod (L21.9).

4. **Its lifetime is bound to attach.** An interactive process with no attached terminal or browser for the platform default `detach_grace` is warned and released, unless started with `--keep`, which holds it to `interactive_max` at most (ADR 0020 decision 7 as revised by L34.3). In its terminal, job-control keys go to the remote foreground job: `^Z` stops the job and `jobs` says the allocation still counts; detaching is `bg`; only `exit` or `kill` releases the allocation.

5. **Idle is measured on what it holds.** Idle is read from the computer's telemetry and the attach channel for each resource the process holds: accelerator use, terminal input, kernel activity. It is never inferred from requests, so an open browser tab is not activity. After `interactive_idle` the process is warned and released. `ps` and the default prompt show its price per hour while it runs.

6. **Every end is warned, and the end runs a state policy.** An interactive process ends by `exit`, `kill`, `detach_grace`, `interactive_idle`, `interactive_max`, the computer's wall time, a drain, a preemption or an out-of-memory kill. The planned ends raise `expiring` (ADR 0027 decision 3) to the attached people's terminals and sessions at `interactive_warn` before them; a drain, a preemption notice or an out-of-memory kill is told to them at once, with the time left. Interactive processes are never placed on preemptible capacity unless `--spot` asks. At every end the declared state policy runs: the application saves open notebooks to the home device through its own interface. A kernel's memory is not checkpointed (L34.16).

7. **It is placed small, near its data, and within a quota.** An interactive request gets the smallest accelerator share the computer offers (a MIG slice, a time-sliced share) in a pool without domains (ADR 0028 decision 8), unless `--gpu=whole`. It is placed near its data and within the residency of the person's primary group, not near the person; `explain` prints the expected round trip from the person's gateway. It prints the scheduler's estimated start before it waits, and `--wait` bounds the wait, after which it ends with exit 3 and the estimate. Interactive work ranks above pipelines and batch (the command map's `nice`), within the platform default `interactive_quota` per group, which is how larger interactive limits are given.

8. **What it reads is recorded, and what it writes is marked.** Each input an interactive process reads is recorded with its snapshot and time in `/sys/proc/<pid>/inputs` (as ADR 0029 decision 4 pins a stage's). Anything it writes carries the lineage `interactive`, the people attached and those inputs; it is never a cache hit (ADR 0001 amendment 1), and `release` refuses it until a batch stage reproduces it. The home device carries a residency and is mounted only on computers in it; a notebook saved by a process that read a `class=restricted` mount is marked restricted, and leaving the home device with it runs the scrub of ADR 0007 amendment 5 and strips its outputs.

9. **Launches from inside go through the person attached.** The Convoy client inside an interactive process speaks through the attach channel of the person attached, so a process launched from a terminal or a notebook cell is launched by that person's session with that person as the actor (ADR 0016 decision 4), never by the group's service identity. It inherits the interactive process's contract (ADR 0004 amendment 3) and outlives it as an ordinary process. With nobody attached, a launch is refused with exit 3.

10. **A platform hub is the driver, and use outside Convoy is counted.** Where the platform runs a notebook hub on a computer, the hub is that computer's driver for browser applications: Convoy asks it to spawn, reads its profiles and its culler as the computer's interactive policy, never overrides them, and never runs a second spawner. On a computer with an allocation, work under the allocation that Convoy did not submit is read from the scheduler and shown in `cost` as `outside Convoy`, by user where the scheduler names one.

## Declined

| Proposal | Why not | What replaces it |
|---|---|---|
| a notebook as a unit | a unit idles on requests and serves many; a kernel serves one person and is idle by every request measure | the `interactive` class (decision 1) |
| `exec` into a pod, or port-forward, as the way in | no grant, no record, a token or a pod name is the key (L21.9) | the attach channel and the gateway (decision 3) |
| a notebook's own token as its login | a token in a URL travels to anyone | the platform login at the gateway (decision 3) |
| checkpointing a kernel's memory | not generally possible across nodes, drivers and GPUs | the state policy, files and lineage (decision 6; L34.16) |
| interactive time without a bound, charged back | charge-back does not return a GPU held by a closed laptop | attach, idle and `interactive_max` (decisions 4, 5) |
| placing the process near the person | data and residency weigh more than echo time | near the data, with the round trip printed (decision 7; L34.19) |

## Consequences

- L34.1 is drafted here, and the open items on interactive work in ADR 0025 and ADR 0029 are settled by it. US-34's other findings land on this record when US-34 is applied: L34.2 (idle on held resources) in decision 5, L34.3 (an amendment to ADR 0020 decision 7 that decision 4 assumes) and L34.4 (job control) in decision 4, L34.5 and L34.11 (gateway, grants) in decision 3, L34.6, L34.7 and L34.18 (home residency, inputs, lineage) in decision 8, L34.8 and L34.15 (ends told and warned) in decision 6, L34.9 and L34.14 (wait, small shares) in decision 7, L34.10 and L34.13 (outside use, the hub) in decision 10, L34.12 (launches) in decision 9, L34.17 (`batch` refusal) in decision 2; L34.16 and L34.19 are accepted.
- ADR 0025 decision 2's `stage` field gains the `interactive` shape. ADR 0020 decision 5's attach channel carries interactive terminals, and decision 7 is amended by L34.3. ADR 0019 decision 7's gateway serves interactive applications at a path per process. ADR 0013's `run` gains `-it`, `--app` and `--keep`, and `attach` gains `--grant`. US-04's notebook unit reads as `run -it --app=jupyter`; L4.3's unit idle rule is unchanged for units. ADR 0028 decision 8's `domain_reserve` keeps interactive work out of domain pools.
- New paths: `/sys/proc/<pid>/inputs`, `/sys/proc/<pid>/attached`. New platform defaults: `detach_grace`, `interactive_idle`, `interactive_max`, `interactive_warn`, `interactive_quota`. New `caps`: `interactive:<shape>` per computer, `hub` where a notebook hub drives browser applications. New executable annotation: `interactive`.
- ROADMAP: `run -it` terminals, job control, attach grants and warnings in the terminal are M2; placement by share, near data, and the start estimate are M3; priority, quotas and release on idle or detach are M6; gateway login for applications and launches as the attached person are M7; drain notices to attached people and allocation use outside Convoy are M8; the hub driver and the Slurm and VM interactive shapes are M9; gateway paths per process are M11.
- Open, to be settled against this record: graphical desktops on GPU nodes (VNC and similar); several people editing one kernel at once; an agent (ADR 0014) driving a notebook; a story that probes this record.
