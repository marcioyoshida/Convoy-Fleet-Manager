# ADR 0023: Metrics, logs and traces are devices the shell reads; Convoy never becomes a monitoring system

**Status:** Draft · 2026-11-11
**Supersedes / relates to:** rule L3.5 (`caps: metrics` for metric conditions); ADR 0009 (stderr is a log) and its amendment 1; ADR 0011 decisions 7 and 9 and amendments 1, 3 and 4 (`/sys`, device classes, drivers from packages, residency, `disconnected`); ADR 0012 (logs and queues are devices) and rule L12.4 (lag from the broker); ADR 0015 (tables and catalogs, a bounded read pins a snapshot); ADR 0001 amendment 1 (`deterministic: false`); ADR 0002 (classes, `compiles_to`); ADR 0007 amendment 5 decision 6 (`caps: log-delete`, `log-forwards`); ADR 0013 decisions 3 and 5 (`explain`, `wait`, `top`, `netstat`); ADR 0016 decisions 3 and 11 (groups, residency); ADR 0018 (labels); ADR 0021 (health collectors) and rule L23.6 (collector versions); rules L4.2 and L7.2 (self-reported metrics, declared and settled)

## Context

The design reads telemetry in a dozen places and never says where it comes from. `wait --healthy --error-rate-below=0.5%` needs `caps: metrics` (L3.5) and nothing more specific. `top` shows GPU utilization and burn rate from `/sys`. Node health reads DCGM, EDAC and filesystem probes through collectors (ADR 0021). `netstat` counts dropped flows where `caps: flows` exists (L24.10). `cost` reads OpenCost. A process's stderr is the container log (ADR 0009), which the kubelet rotates at 10 MiB and deletes with the pod, after which the lines live, if anywhere, in a log store the platform runs. The secret scrub already treats log stores as things with capabilities (ADR 0007 amendment 5 decision 6). None of these reads names a store, an identity, a residency, a freshness or a price.

Facts that constrain the design:

- Telemetry lives in stores the platform already runs and other tools already read: Prometheus, Thanos or Mimir, CloudWatch, Azure Monitor and Datadog for metrics; Loki, Elasticsearch or OpenSearch and CloudWatch Logs for logs; Tempo, Jaeger and X-Ray for traces; usually behind an OpenTelemetry collector. Convoy must read them where they are. A second scrape or a second store would double the cost and disagree with the dashboards on-call already trusts.
- Recent telemetry is not settled. Samples arrive late and out of order, log lines are batched by agents, and a store's compactor and downsampler rewrite old data, so the same query over the same range can return different results minutes or weeks apart.
- An absent series is not a zero. A service with no 5xx series has either served no errors or reported nothing, and an error-rate condition that reads absence as zero passes a canary that is not running.
- Query cost is real and shaped differently per store: per gigabyte scanned for CloudWatch Logs Insights, per query or per series for some SaaS stores, compute and memory for a self-hosted Prometheus, where one high-cardinality query can take the store down for everyone.
- Logs carry personal data and secrets (ADR 0007 amendment 5), so a log store has a residency as binding as a database's. Metrics usually do not, but their label values can.
- A multi-tenant store separates tenants by a header or an account (Mimir and Loki `X-Scope-OrgID`, a CloudWatch account), or by label enforcement in a proxy. Without either, anyone who can query sees every namespace.

## Decision

1. **Telemetry stores are devices in three classes.** `/dev/metrics/`, `/dev/logs/` and `/dev/traces/` are device classes under ADR 0011 decision 9. Each device's `by-id` key is the store's own identity (the Prometheus or Mimir cluster's external label set, the CloudWatch account and region, the Loki tenant URL). `by-provider`, `by-topology` and `by-label` are views; `by-topology` says which clusters a store covers, which is how the session finds the store for a given mount. A driver comes from a package (ADR 0011 amendment 1) and reports its dialect and capabilities in `caps`: `metrics:promql`, `metrics:cloudwatch`, `logs:logql`, `logs:opensearch`, `traces:traceql`, plus `label-enforce`, `log-delete` and `log-forwards`. A device is mounted with an identity, a tenant and a residency like any other:

   ```
   mount -o identity=role:orders-observer,tenant=orders,residency=eu,text=tsv,ro \
         /dev/metrics/by-id/mimir-eu-1 /metrics/eu
   ```

   The platform names a default store per topology for each class (`metrics_device`, `log_device`, `trace_device`), mounted in every session for the clusters the session can reach, so `wait`, `top` and `netstat` need no `mount` first.

2. **A path is a selector, and a read is bounded.** Under a metrics mount, `ls` lists metric names, `ls <metric>/` lists label names, and a path whose segments are label matchers is a series selector: `/metrics/eu/http_requests_total/unit=orders-api/code=~5..`. Under a logs mount the segments are stream labels (`/logs/eu/namespace=orders/unit=orders-api`); under a traces mount they are a service or a trace id (`/traces/eu/service=orders-api`, `/traces/eu/id=4bf9…`). `cat`, `head` and `tail -n` read records as typed rows (time, labels, value or line or span) in the mount's `text=` form (ADR 0012 decision 2), over a range given by `--since`, `--until` and, for metrics, `--step`. A read with no range is refused, not defaulted, because "everything" is a query that costs whatever the store says it costs. `explain` prints the store, the dialect query the selector compiled to, the range resolved to absolute times, and the price.

3. **A bounded read pins its range, and only settled data is cached.** At plan time the range's end is resolved to an absolute time no later than now minus the store's reported ingestion delay, which `/sys/metrics/<id>/` carries. The read's artifact id is the hash of the device, the compiled query, the absolute range, the step and the driver version. A range that ends inside the store's `settle` window (late samples, agent batching), or that overlaps data the store will compact or downsample, is `deterministic: false` (ADR 0001 amendment 1): its id is salted and it is never served from cache. A range the store reports as settled and immutable is cacheable, and `replay` of it reads the same data while the store retains it. The receipt records the range, the settle state and the store's retention, so a reader knows when a replay will stop being possible.

4. **Absence is `unknown`, never a value.** A condition or a computed column that reads a selector with no series, or a series whose last sample is older than twice its scrape interval, reads `unknown`. `wait --error-rate-below=<pct>` passes only when the rate's denominator series exists and is fresh and the numerator is below the bound, where a missing numerator with a fresh denominator is zero; a stale or missing denominator keeps the condition unmet, `wait` keeps waiting, and at `--timeout` it exits 2 with `source unknown: <selector>, last sample <age>`. `top`, `ps` and `netstat` print `?` for an unknown reading with its age, never 0 and never the last value as if current. Every reading Convoy shows carries its store, its collector and the collector's version (rule L23.6).

5. **Conditions name their query, and units declare theirs.** A unit's annotation or its record may declare named conditions, `slo: error_rate = <selector expression>` and `slo: latency_p99 = …`, which `wait --healthy` and `release --canary` evaluate by name (`--slo=error_rate<0.5%`). The platform provides defaults for common shapes (an HTTP server behind the gateway, a queue consumer) from the gateway's and the broker's own series; the built-in `--error-rate-below` is the gateway default. `explain` prints the compiled query of every condition, the store it reads and the freshness, and refuses a condition whose store is not reachable from the target mount's topology (L3.5 becomes this decision).

6. **Convoy reads; it never writes the monitoring configuration.** Convoy does not scrape, store, alert or draw. It adds labels so that what it runs can be found: every object it creates carries `convoy.io/pid`, the owning group and the unit name (ADR 0018), and the platform's collectors keep them, so `ps` and a metrics selector join on the same keys. Recording rules, alerts, dashboards and collector pipelines belong to the platform's observability repository. A unit that needs a scrape target or a dashboard declares it in its package, and the platform's GitOps applies it as intent (ADR 0010 amendment 1). `logger` keeps its mapping (a Kubernetes Event and an audit line) and also writes one line to the mount's log device where `caps` allows writes. Pushes of arbitrary series from the shell are not offered; a stage's own numbers stay in its receipt as self-reported metrics (rules L4.2 and L7.2).

7. **A process's stderr is one path across its life.** `cat` and `tail -f` on `/proc/<pid>/fd/2` read the pod's log while the pod exists and the platform's log device after it, under the same selector (the pod's labels and `convoy.io/pid`), joined in order. The read says where each part came from and counts the gap where the kubelet rotated lines before the agent shipped them, from the agent's own drop counters where `caps` has them and as `unknown` where it does not. A receipt names the log device and selector that hold its stderr, so `cat` of a finished process's stderr after the pod is deleted reads the store, within the store's retention, which `explain` prints. This settles where ADR 0009's "stderr is a log" goes after the pod.

8. **Tenancy and residency are the device's, and the shell enforces nothing it cannot prove.** A mount opens the store under the mount's identity and tenant. Where the store separates tenants by header or account, the group's tenant is the mount option and the store enforces it. Where it separates them by labels, `caps: label-enforce` names the proxy that rewrites every query to the namespaces the group owns; without it the device is readable by the platform group only, and `mount` by anyone else is refused with the reason, because a shell-side filter on a store that returns everything is a filter the next `curl` skips. Log and trace devices carry their residency, and a read from a session of another residency is refused (ADR 0016 decision 11); metrics devices carry one too, and the platform may mark a metrics device `residency=aggregate-export` to allow reads of aggregated series across residencies, which `explain` prints.

9. **Queries are priced and bounded before they run.** Telemetry is a fourth cost shape after compute, queues (ADR 0012 decision 8) and tables (ADR 0015 decision 8). The driver estimates a read from the store's own statistics before running it: series count and points for metrics (from the store's cardinality endpoint, or a `count` query at a fraction of the cost), bytes in range for logs (from the index or the stream statistics), spans for traces. `explain` prints the estimate and the price shape from `/sys/<class>/<id>/price`. A read above the platform's `telemetry_query_max` (series, bytes or price) is refused at plan time naming the limit and the narrower selector that fits; `--budget` binds as for any process. The receipt records estimated and actual, and actual comes from the store's response statistics where it reports them.

10. **Text tools run unchanged; the store only narrows.** A text tool over a log path reads lines, and the real `grep`, `awk` and `sort` run over them (ADR 0001). Where a log driver can push a filter down, `grep -F <string>` and the path's stream labels become the store's line filter to cut the bytes read, and the real `grep` still runs over what comes back, so the result is exactly what `grep` would print over the full range. Regular expressions are never pushed down, because the stores' dialects (RE2, Lucene) do not have POSIX semantics. Metric reads produce rows that text tools and typed verbs read like any `typed` input (ADR 0002); a rate, a quantile or a sum over labels is either part of the selector (`rate`, `sum by` written in the store's dialect as the path's final segment, `explain` printing it) or a typed verb over the rows, never a text tool compiled into PromQL.

11. **Verbs on the devices.**

    | Verb | On a telemetry device | Rule |
    |---|---|---|
    | `ls`, `ls -l` | metric names, label names, log streams, services; with series count, last sample, retention and ingest delay | from `/sys/<class>/<id>/`, refreshed on the inventory cadence; `ls --fresh` asks the store |
    | `stat` | a metric's type, unit and help; a stream's labels and rate; a trace's span count and duration | the store's metadata API |
    | `cat`, `head`, `tail -n`, `< path` | bounded read over `--since`/`--until` | decisions 2 and 3; no range is refused |
    | `tail -f` | follow: new samples at `--step`, new log lines, new traces | the opt-in stream rules of ADR 0001; never cached |
    | `grep`, `awk`, `sort`, `wc` | over the rows or lines a read returns | decision 10; `grep -F` narrows the read |
    | `du`, `df` | series and bytes by group, retention, price shape | where the store reports them per tenant or label |
    | `wait --slo`, `release --canary` | named conditions | decisions 4 and 5 |
    | `top`, `ps`, `netstat` | live columns from the default devices | decision 4: `?` with an age when unknown |
    | `logger` | a line to the log device, an Event and an audit line | decision 6 |
    | `rm`, `>`, `tee` | refused | telemetry is written by collectors; deletion is `secret scrub` through `caps: log-delete` (ADR 0007 amendment 5) |

12. **Disconnected stores and edge sites.** A store unreachable from the session is `disconnected` (ADR 0011 amendment 4): `ls` shows its last inventory with its age, a bounded read is refused or degraded per the plan (exit 0 with the degraded flag when a union read misses one store), and conditions that read it are `unknown`, so a release waiting on an edge site's error rate waits, it does not pass. An edge site whose collector buffers locally reports its buffered range, and a read of a range not yet shipped says so.

## Declined

| Proposal | Why not | What replaces it |
|---|---|---|
| a Convoy scrape and time-series store | doubles cost, disagrees with the dashboards on-call trusts, and makes Convoy part of the monitoring stack it should read | devices over the platform's stores (decision 1) |
| alerting rules, dashboards and recording rules written by the shell | configuration with no owner outside the session; drift from the observability repository | packages declare them; GitOps applies them as intent (decision 6) |
| translating `grep`, `awk` and `sort` into LogQL or PromQL | dialect semantics differ (RE2, Lucene, staleness rules); results would differ from Linux | `grep -F` narrows the read and the real tool decides (decision 10) |
| an unbounded `cat` of a metric or a stream, defaulting to "the last hour" | a hidden default is a hidden cost and a hidden answer | a range is required (decision 2) |
| reading absent series as zero | a canary that is not running passes | `unknown` (decision 4) |
| shell-side tenant filtering on a store that returns every namespace | the filter is advisory; the store's API is reachable without it | `label-enforce` or platform-only (decision 8) |
| pushing metrics from shell stages to the store | unbounded cardinality from scripts; self-reported numbers mixed with measured ones | receipt metrics, flagged self-reported (L4.2, L7.2) |
| caching every telemetry read like a pipe artifact | recent data changes after the read | only settled, immutable ranges are cached (decision 3) |

## Consequences

- Rule L3.5 is replaced by decision 5: a metric condition names a store reachable from the target's topology, not a bare capability. `wait --error-rate-below` keeps its spelling as the gateway default; `--slo=<name><op><value>` is a new `wait` and `release` flag (ADR 0013 decision 11 requires it in the flag tables, which a later revision of decision 4 carries). `top`, `ps`, `netstat` and `cost` read the default devices and show `?` with an age when unknown.
- ADR 0009's stderr gains its second half (decision 7): the receipt names the log device and selector, and a finished process's stderr is readable within the store's retention. The node health collectors of ADR 0021 read through metrics devices, so their readings carry a store and a version like every other.
- New device classes `/dev/metrics`, `/dev/logs`, `/dev/traces`. New `caps` keys: the dialects (`metrics:promql`, `metrics:cloudwatch`, `logs:logql`, `logs:opensearch`, `traces:traceql`), `label-enforce`, and log writes for `logger`; `log-delete` and `log-forwards` exist already (ADR 0007 amendment 5). New platform defaults: `metrics_device`, `log_device` and `trace_device` per topology, `telemetry_query_max`, the settle window per store, `residency=aggregate-export` on a metrics device. New annotation field: `slo:` on units and images.
- ROADMAP: the three drivers, bounded reads, the settle rule and pricing are M5 with mounts; stderr across the pod's life is M4 with receipts; `wait --slo`, unit conditions and `release --canary` on them are M11; `top`, `ps` and `netstat` columns with `unknown` are M8; tenancy, `label-enforce` and residency are M7.
- Open, to be settled against this ADR: exemplars and the join from a trace to the receipt of the process that served it; continuous profiling stores as a fourth class; where `caps: flows` comes from (eBPF agents, VPC flow logs) and whether it is a metrics device; the cost of `ls -l` inventories on stores that price metadata calls. [US-26](../user-stories/US-26-the-dashboard-said-green.md) probes this record under the SRE persona with an incident across seven stores; it proposes revisions to decisions 3, 5, 7, 8 and 10 (L26.2, L26.1, L26.12, L26.5, L26.9), ten rules and one accepted limitation (L26.4, residency and the reader's location), not yet applied.
