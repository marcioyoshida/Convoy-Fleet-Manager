# ADR 0032: A stream reads a pinned manifest through a window, at the rate its stage needs

**Status:** Draft · 2026-11-14
**Supersedes / relates to:** open finding L36.1 (an input too large to stage, read as a moving window, has no manifest, window, rate, partitioning, position or completeness model); ADR 0029 decisions 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8 and its open item on inputs too large to stage (file systems and reach, in place or staged, pinned sources, verified copies, residency, cleanup); ADR 0001 and its amendment 1 (pipes are staged artifacts, `|=` is opt-in; non-deterministic stages); ADR 0012 decision 2 (a follow on a topic); the pipeline compiler §8.5; ADR 0002 (executable annotations); ADR 0003 (partitions); ADR 0004 amendments 1, 2 and 3 (gang restart from checkpoint, the degraded channel, the inherited contract); ADR 0016 decisions 4, 5 and 7 (the exchange per open, the group's service identity, grants by group); ADR 0017 decision 2 (gates from policy); ADR 0023 (telemetry the shell reads); ADR 0025 decisions 2, 4 and 8 (`bounds`, placement, operators); ADR 0026 (pins); ADR 0027 (events); ADR 0028 decision 9 (`/dev/shm` caches); the command map rows for `cat`, `du` and `fuser`

## Context

ADR 0029 gave every input two ways to reach a stage: read it in place where the computer can reach it, or stage a copy onto the computer's own file system. Some inputs fit neither. A training set of 1.4 PB in 40 million objects cannot be staged onto any file system a job will get, and on a computer that cannot reach the store it cannot be read in place either; even where it can, reading in place says nothing about whether the store, the link and the relays can feed 32 GPUs, or what four epochs of it cost. Data-loader libraries stream such sets already, from object stores or tar shards, with a local cache and a prefetch queue; Convoy has never described what they do, and so cannot plan, price, resume, count or clean it up.

US-36 ran a 32-GPU training run through that gap: no plan on a computer that could neither reach nor hold the data; a manifest built by listing for three hours; GPUs starved at 35 % for four days; 32 loader workers each reading the whole set; the loader's cache filling root disks; the data owner's production readers throttled; a credential that expired twelve hours in; a lifecycle policy archiving prefixes under the run; a restart that re-read 40 % of an epoch; a window serving frames re-encoded since; 91 % of the data seen and exit 0; seven terabytes of restricted frames left on node disks; an unrecorded shuffle seed; one relay down and half the ranks starved; and `cat` of the whole set into a pipe.

Facts that constrain the design:

- An object store's own version ids and ETags identify objects; recomputing content hashes over a petabyte at plan time is not possible, and listing tens of millions of objects takes hours where an inventory is a file.
- A stream's useful rate is the least of the store's share for this reader, the link, the relays and the window's disk; GPUs idle when it is below what they consume.
- Reader libraries keep their own caches and skip what they cannot read unless told otherwise.
- A multi-day stream outlives any token exchanged for a session.
- A store shared with production readers has a request rate that a single stream can take most of.

## Decision

1. **`streamed` is the third way an input reaches a stage.** ADR 0029 decision 2's choice gains `streamed` (as revised by L36.15): the planner streams an input when it exceeds what the computer can hold per task or the platform default `stage_max`, or when the computer can reach it only through relays, or when the stage reads it a bounded number of times and staging would cost more; `explain` prints the choice and the reason. For a streamed input the capacity check is on the window, not the input, and the rate check of decision 4 applies. Streams cover device reads (object store prefixes, table snapshots); topics keep ADR 0012's follow. In a pipeline, `|=` from a device read is a stream over a pinned manifest and produces no artifact, and a plain `|` whose source read exceeds `stage_max` is refused with exit 3 and the `|=` spelling.

2. **A stream reads a pinned manifest.** A stream's manifest lists every object it will read with the store's version id and ETag, which are its content ids; above `list_max` objects it is built from the store's inventory, never by listing. Its id is the hash of the manifest, and it is pinned for the run (ADR 0029 decision 4). The plan checks the storage class of every object: objects in an archive class are restored first as a planned, priced step, or the plan is refused. The stream holds a read pin on its manifest for the run, which the store's lifecycle honours where the store supports it (as ADR 0026 pins images), and `explain` warns where it does not. `/sys/proc/<pid>/streams/<n>/manifest` holds it.

3. **The window is a device of the process.** A stream reads through a window: an `ephemeral` file system on the computer (ADR 0029 decision 1) or a `/dev/shm` cache (ADR 0028 decision 9), with a declared size and prefetch depth, shown in `/sys/proc/<pid>/streams/<n>/window`. Its entries are keyed by the manifest's content ids and verified; a new manifest invalidates every entry whose id changed. The platform toolbox points reader libraries' own caches at the window, and writes elsewhere count against the process's ephemeral bound. The window is encrypted when the data is `class=restricted`, is never shared across groups, and is removed when the process ends, however it ends.

4. **A stream has a rate, and placement checks it.** The rate a streamed stage needs comes from its executable's annotation (`reads:` bytes per sample and samples per second per accelerator, ADR 0002) or from a measured sample run; the rate a computer can give is the least of the source device's share for this reader, the link, the relays and the window's disk. In ADR 0025 decision 4's filter, computers below the needed rate times the platform default `stream_margin` are removed before ranking. `ps` shows the achieved rate and the accelerators' idle share; a stream below its stage's rate floor shows `starved` with the reason. Rate estimates come from earlier receipts and are marked as such (L36.17).

5. **A stream is partitioned across its process tree, and its order is pinned.** The plan partitions a stream's manifest across the members of the process tree that reads it (ranks, and loader workers within each rank); each member reads its partition, reads outside it are counted, and the receipt records the partition map. The shuffle seed is pinned and recorded with the partition map; a stream without them is `deterministic: false` (ADR 0001 amendment 1).

6. **A stream's position is part of the checkpoint.** With `--checkpoint`, each checkpoint records every stream's position: manifest id, partition map, epoch, shuffle seed and offset per partition. A restart, including a gang restart from checkpoint (ADR 0004 amendment 1), resumes each stream exactly there, so no part of an epoch is read twice or skipped.

7. **What a stream read is counted.** Shards read and skipped are counted per epoch against the manifest, by the reader library through the toolbox where it can report them and by the window where it cannot. Below the stage's floor the stage exits 1; at or above it, 0 degraded (ADR 0004 amendment 2). The receipt lists the skipped shards per epoch.

8. **A stream crosses a computer's reach through the relays it declares.** Where a computer reaches a store only through relays (transfer nodes, a proxy), they are declared in its `reach` (ADR 0029 decision 1) with their rates and operator. A stream spreads across every declared relay and fails over when one goes; below its rate floor it is `starved`. Relays the computer's operator runs are used as they are (ADR 0025 decision 8), and their transfer is priced.

9. **A stream's credential is the controller's to keep.** The credential a stream reads with is refreshed by the controller through the group's service identity (ADR 0016 decision 5) for the process's lifetime, independent of any session. A refresh that fails pauses the stream and raises an event to the owning group (ADR 0027) before the credential expires.

10. **A stream is priced, and a shared source is asked.** `explain` prices a stream's requests and bytes per epoch and in total, in the source's and the computer's units. Above `small_object_max` objects it warns and offers a packed form: shards by content id, produced once as a staged artifact and streamed from then on. It shows the stream's share of the source device's declared rate; above the platform default `stream_share` a stream from another group's device needs that group's approval (ADR 0017 decision 2), and the owner sees the stream in `fuser` on the device. Each epoch is read and priced again; a stream is not cached beyond its window (L36.16).

## Declined

| Proposal | Why not | What replaces it |
|---|---|---|
| staging a dataset in pieces, by hand | forgotten, unverified, unpriced, never cleaned up | the window (decision 3) |
| a shared cluster cache holding whole datasets across epochs | capacity, residency and restricted data across groups | a window per process, and staging where an input fits (decisions 1, 3) |
| content-hashing every object at plan time | hours over a petabyte; the store already identifies its objects | a manifest of version ids from the inventory (decision 2) |
| letting reader libraries skip what they cannot read | a model trained on 91 % with exit 0 | counted completeness against a floor (decision 7) |
| streams on the session's token | it expires long before the run | the controller's credential (decision 9) |
| unbounded reads from another group's store | they throttle the owner's production | a share and the owner's approval (decision 10) |

## Consequences

- L36.1 is drafted here, and ADR 0029's open item on inputs too large to stage is settled by it. US-36's other findings land on this record when US-36 is applied: L36.3 and L36.15 (pipes; an amendment to ADR 0029 decision 2 that decision 1 assumes) in decision 1, L36.2 and L36.18 (archive classes and pins, inventory manifests) in decision 2, L36.7, L36.10 and L36.12 (window entries, reader caches, cleanup) in decision 3, L36.14 (rate) in decision 4, L36.13 and L36.19 (partitions, seed) in decision 5, L36.4 (position) in decision 6, L36.8 (completeness) in decision 7, L36.9 (relays) in decision 8, L36.5 (credential) in decision 9, L36.6 and L36.11 (price, share) in decision 10; L36.16 and L36.17 are accepted.
- ADR 0029 decision 2 gains `streamed`. ADR 0001's `|=` extends from a channel between stages to a read of a device's manifest; ADR 0012 decision 2's follow is unchanged for topics. ADR 0004 amendment 1's checkpoints carry stream positions. ADR 0028 decision 9's `/dev/shm` caches can hold windows. ADR 0002's annotations gain `reads:` and `skips:`.
- New paths: `/sys/proc/<pid>/streams/<n>/` with `manifest`, `window`, `rate`, `partitions` and `read`. New platform defaults: `stage_max`, `list_max`, `stream_margin`, `stream_share`, `small_object_max`. New `caps`: `inventory` and `read-pin` on object-store devices, relays in a computer's `reach`.
- ROADMAP: the choice of `streamed`, rate in placement and partitioning are M3; inventory manifests, window entries by content id, positions in checkpoints and packed forms are M4; window devices, read pins and archive restores are M5; the controller's stream credentials and the share approval are M7; completeness counting, `starved` and relay failover are M8; reader-library shims in the toolbox and inventory drivers are M9.
- Open, to be settled against this record: outputs too large to stage (checkpoint shards written as a stream); streams from live sources into training (sensors, edge devices); several processes sharing one stream's window; a story that probes this record.
