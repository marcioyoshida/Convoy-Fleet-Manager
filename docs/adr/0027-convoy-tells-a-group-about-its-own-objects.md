# ADR 0027: Convoy tells a group about its own objects, through the channels the platform already has

**Status:** Draft · 2026-11-13 · rules from user story US-31 added 2026-11-13
**Supersedes / relates to:** open finding L31.1 (Convoy's own events have no channel, route, subscription or event model); ADR 0017 decisions 2, 4, 5 and 7 (`page:<route>` at a gate's timeout, `caps: alerting`, answers from the approver's session); ADR 0021 decisions 5 and 8 (the health controller "pages"); ADR 0023 decision 6 (Convoy never alerts, never writes the monitoring configuration); ADR 0016 decisions 5, 7, 9, 10 and 11 (groups own unattended work, approvals by a group, lifecycle, `/etc/fstab.d/<group>`, the session's residency); ADR 0020 decisions 1 and 2 (the controller owns what outlives a session, the journal); ADR 0004 and its amendments 2 and 3 (the degraded channel, the inherited contract); ADR 0009 amendment 1 (receipts spooled at a site); ADR 0011 decisions 2, 7 and 9 and amendments 3 and 4 (views, `/sys`, the same rules for every class, `residency`, `disconnected`); ADR 0013 decisions 1, 4 and 5 (the closed verb set, contract flags, `wait`); ADR 0007 amendment 2 (the scrub); ADR 0024 (installed by Terraform); the command map rows for `mailx`, `write`, `wall` and `mesg`; US-31 findings L31.2 to L31.17

## Context

Convoy waits on people and finishes work for people. A gate suspends a release until a group answers (ADR 0017); an unattended run fails at 4 a.m.; a run lands on its floor and exits degraded (ADR 0004 amendment 2); a budget runs out; a hold, a branch unit, a lend, a grant or a vulnerability exception (ADR 0026 decision 8) expires; an edge site loses its link (ADR 0011 amendment 4); the health controller cordons a node or stops at its budget (ADR 0021). Each of these is a state of an object Convoy owns, and each matters only if someone hears about it.

The design names the act and never the means. ADR 0017 names `page:<route>` for a gate's timeout and `caps: alerting`, but nothing for the request itself; ADR 0021 says the controller "pages" without a route; the command map maps `mailx` to "a platform notifier". ADR 0023 decision 6 says Convoy never alerts and never writes the monitoring configuration, which is right for telemetry and silent about Convoy's own events. US-31 spent a weekend in that gap: an approval request nobody received, a health route that had failed for three weeks, an exception that lapsed unannounced, a run's failure buried under 1 900 partition messages with stderr in the mail, an approval given by a chat bot's token, a message still saying "awaiting approval" after a rollback, and a timeout counted from before anyone could know.

Facts that constrain the design:

- Every platform already has the channels: a chat workspace, a mail relay, an incident tool that owns on-call schedules and escalation policies (PagerDuty, Opsgenie, incident.io), and often an Alertmanager whose routes are configuration in a GitOps repository. A second schedule in Convoy would disagree with the first.
- Channels deliver at least once and on a best-effort basis; some edit a message in place, some cannot; some can carry an interactive answer, most cannot carry an identity the identity provider vouches for.
- A message is data at rest in a third party's service, in that service's region, under its retention (structural misroute S13).
- People leave, are on holiday and sleep; groups persist (ADR 0016 decision 9).

## Decision

1. **Channels are devices under `/dev/notify/`.** The `by-id` key is the provider's own identifier (a chat workspace and channel id, an incident tool's service id, a mail identity and region, a webhook's registered name), with views by kind: `chat`, `mail`, `incident`, `webhook`, and `session`, the built-in channel to users' session journals (ADR 0020 decision 2). Channels are installed by a module (ADR 0024) or registered with `iac: none`. `/sys/notify/<id>/` holds the residency and retention of what the channel stores, its price file, whether it edits messages in place, whether it may carry answers (`act=none|approve`, decision 8), its rate limit and its state: `ok`, or `failing` with the last success and the last error.

2. **A group's route maps urgency to channels, and the platform has a default and a fallback.** `/etc/notify.d/<group>` is the group's route, published like the mount profile of ADR 0016 decision 10:

   ```
   page    incident:pd-release          # the incident tool resolves who is on call
   notify  chat:slack-acme-C04RELEASE
   digest  mail:ses-us-east-1-release   hours=09:00-18:00 America/Sao_Paulo
   ```

   `/etc/notify.d/default` serves groups with no route, and `/etc/notify.d/fallback` receives what a failing channel could not deliver. A route only names channels; schedules, escalation steps and alert rules stay in the incident tool or in its GitOps configuration, and Convoy never creates or edits them. A route kept in a GitOps repository is changed as intent there (ADR 0010 amendment 1).

3. **Events are a closed set, raised only by Convoy's own objects.** The classes are: `approval.requested`, `approval.answered`, `approval.void`, `approval.expired`; `proc.done`, `proc.failed`, `proc.degraded`; `budget.threshold`, `budget.exhausted`, `deadline.near`; `refused.unattended`; `expiring`, `expired`; `device.disconnected`, `device.reconnected`; `node.action` and `node.stopped` (ADR 0021); `release.rolledback`; `route.failing`. Adding a class amends this record, as adding a verb amends ADR 0013 decision 1. Each class has a default urgency, `page`, `notify` or `digest`, which the platform may tighten per policy: `page` for an `approval.requested` with less than the platform's `page_before` left, for `refused.unattended`, `budget.exhausted`, `node.stopped` and `route.failing`; `notify` for completions, failures, degraded runs and answered approvals; `digest` for the rest. Convoy never raises an event from telemetry: a condition on a series is the monitoring system's to alert on (ADR 0023 decision 6), and a named condition that a Convoy process waits on raises only that process's events.

4. **Every event goes to a group, and people subscribe on top.** An event is addressed to the group that owns the object (ADR 0016 decision 3) through the group's route; an `approval.requested` goes to the approving group's route, and its author is never notified as an approver. A person may add a subscription for themselves; no event is ever addressed only to a person, so an offboarding never silences it. A group with no route uses the default route; an event is never dropped for want of one.

5. **Subscriptions are on process trees and objects, and children roll up.** `--notify=<group>[:<class>…]` is a contract flag (ADR 0013 decision 4) on every creating verb: it adds a group's route to the root process's events, and applies to the whole tree, so child processes and partitions raise no events of their own and the root's event summarises them (partitions failed, the floor met or not, the degraded flag). `wait --notify[=<group>] <subject>` turns a wait into a subscription and returns at once (ADR 0013 decision 5). Events are rolled up per object and class, each route has the platform's `notify_max` per hour, and what exceeds it goes to the route's digest.

6. **A message is a pointer to an object, never a copy of its output.** A message carries the object's path, the event class, one status line, the time of the state it reports, the scrubbed `explain` summary (ADR 0007 amendment 2) and a deduplication key derived from the path (`proc/9101`, `unit/payments`), which incident tools use to group it with alerts that carry the same object labels (ADR 0018). It never carries stderr, argument values or data. A channel whose residency does not cover the object's receives the path alone. When the object's state changes, the message is edited in place where the channel can, or a superseding message names the earlier one; the object, not the message, is the record.

7. **Delivery belongs to the controller, and a failure is itself an event.** The notifier runs in the controller (ADR 0020 decision 1) in each residency, so no session needs to be attached for an event to leave. Delivery is at least once; each event's raise and delivery times are written to `/sys/proc/<pid>/events` and to the receipt. A channel that fails moves to `failing`, the event goes to the fallback route, and `route.failing` is raised to the platform group; the inventory sends each channel a test event daily; `explain` of a verb whose events would use a failing route prints it. At a site without its link, events are spooled with the receipts (ADR 0009 amendment 1) and delivered on reconnect with their raise time; the site's own `device.disconnected` is raised by the central controller, which is the side that can see it.

8. **A channel may carry an answer only for an identity the identity provider vouches for.** A channel with `act=approve` maps each of its accounts to the identity provider's user and re-authenticates the user for the answer; such an answer is an answer from that user under ADR 0017 decision 5, and the receipt records the channel. Every other channel carries a link to the object, and the answer is given in a session. A bot's token never answers.

9. **The POSIX messaging utilities are thin views of this.** `mailx -s <subject> <channel-path>|@<group>` sends a free-text message through a channel or a group's route, as the user, scrubbed, priced and rate-limited like any message, and exits 0 when the channel accepts it; delivery is a state, not the exit code. `write <user>` and `wall` deliver to the session channel: to every attached terminal, and to the session journal when the user has none, shown at the next attach and said so by `write`. `mesg n` refuses `write` to one's terminals and never refuses events.

10. **Notification is priced and shown before it happens.** Each channel's price file prices its messages (SMS and voice per message, incident tools per event where they bill so), and each message is charged to the group that owns the object. `explain` prints, for each event the plan can raise, the class, the urgency, the route, the channels and their state, and a price band where messages cost money.

## Declined

| Proposal | Why not | What replaces it |
|---|---|---|
| on-call schedules and escalation policies in Convoy | a second schedule disagrees with the incident tool's | routes name channels; the incident tool resolves who (decision 2) |
| Convoy alerting on telemetry | the monitoring system already does, and two alerting systems double-page | events only from Convoy's own objects (decision 3), with a deduplication key (decision 6) |
| addressing events to people | people leave, sleep and go on holiday | groups own events; people subscribe on top (decision 4) |
| messages that carry output | data at rest in a third party's service and residency | a pointer to the object (decision 6) |
| answers from chat buttons under the app's token | the token is not a person | `act=approve` channels with mapped, re-authenticated accounts, else a link (decision 8) |
| a new verb family for notification | the closed verb set already has `mailx`, `write`, `wall`, `wait` and contract flags | `--notify`, `wait --notify` and the POSIX utilities (decisions 5, 9) |

## Consequences

- L31.1 is drafted here. [US-31](../user-stories/US-31-nobody-was-told.md) was applied on 2026-11-13: L31.5 and L31.9 revised ADR 0017 decisions 5 and 7 inline (answers from channels only for mapped, re-authenticated accounts; timeouts from first delivery), which decisions 7 and 8 assume; its thirteen rules are in the Rules section below, with pointers in ADRs 0004, 0006, 0007, 0009, 0010, 0011, 0013, 0016, 0017, 0018, 0020, 0021, 0023 and 0026; L31.16 is accepted.
- ADR 0017's `page:<route>` names the `page` urgency of a group's route, and `caps: alerting` is an `incident` channel; ADR 0021's pages are `node.action` and `node.stopped` events to the platform group's route; ADR 0023 decision 6 stands, and this record says what it leaves to Convoy: events about its own objects. ADR 0013 decision 4 gains `--notify`, and decision 5 gains `wait --notify`. The command map rows for `mailx`, `write`, `wall` and `mesg` read as decision 9.
- New paths: `/dev/notify/`, `/sys/notify/<id>/`, `/etc/notify.d/<group>`, `default` and `fallback`, `/sys/proc/<pid>/events`. New platform defaults: `page_before`, `notify_max`, `expiry_warn`, working hours per group for digests. New `caps`: `notify:<kind>`, `act=` per channel.
- ROADMAP: `write` and `wall` to the session journal are M2 with the session; raise and delivery times in receipts are M4; the channel drivers and `/sys/notify/` are M5 with mounts; routes per group and answers from mapped accounts are M7; the notifier in the controller per residency, fallback, daily test events and spooled site events are M8; channel driver packages and modules are M9.
- Open, to be settled against this record: conversations beyond answers (asking a chat channel for a process's status, or to run a command); notifying people outside the organisation (a customer status page); per-person quiet hours and language; how a digest is laid out; a story that probes this record.

## Rules · 2026-11-13 · from the user-story probes

Each line is a `rule` finding from `docs/user-stories/README.md` written here as decided; the story holds the reasoning.

- **L31.2** (US-31) a route names a service, a channel or a webhook the platform has; Convoy sends events to it and never creates or edits schedules, escalation policies or alert rules; a route whose definition lives in a GitOps configuration is changed as intent there (decision 2).
- **L31.3** (US-31) `write` to a user with no attached terminal keeps the message in the user's session journal and shows it at the next attach, and `write` says so; `mailx` exits 0 on acceptance by the channel, and delivery is a state in `/sys/notify/<id>/` and `/sys/proc/<pid>/events` (decision 9).
- **L31.4** (US-31) events are addressed to the owning group through its route, never only to a person; a person may subscribe in addition; a group without a route uses `/etc/notify.d/default`, and no event is dropped for want of one; offboarding never silences a group's events (decision 4).
- **L31.6** (US-31) events are rolled up per object and class; each route has the platform's `notify_max` per hour, past which events go to its digest; channels have a price file, and each message is charged to the group that owns the object (decisions 5, 10).
- **L31.7** (US-31) every message carries the object's path and the time of the state it reports; a later state of the same object edits the message in place where the channel can, or sends a superseding message that names the earlier one (decision 6).
- **L31.8** (US-31) a delivery failure is itself an event, sent to the fallback route, and the channel shows `failing` with its last success; `explain` of a verb whose events use a failing route prints it; the inventory sends each channel a test event daily (decision 7).
- **L31.10** (US-31) events raised where the notifier cannot be reached are spooled with the receipts (ADR 0009 amendment 1) and delivered on reconnect with their raise time; a site's disconnection is raised by the central controller (decision 7).
- **L31.11** (US-31) every event carries a deduplication key derived from the object's path, which the incident tool uses to group it with alerts carrying the same object labels (ADR 0018); Convoy raises events only about its own objects and never alerts on telemetry (decisions 3, 6).
- **L31.12** (US-31) an approval request goes to the approving group's route, which the incident tool resolves to whoever is on call; for a quorum above one, a further answer is requested only after each answer; the author is never notified as an approver (decision 4).
- **L31.13** (US-31) a message body is the object's path, the event class, a status line and the scrubbed `explain` summary, never stderr or argument values; each channel carries a residency and a retention, and an event about an object outside the channel's residency is reduced to its path (decision 6).
- **L31.14** (US-31) `--notify=<group>[:<class>…]` on a process applies to its tree; child processes and partitions raise no events of their own, and the root's event summarises them (failed partitions, the floor, the degraded flag) (decision 5).
- **L31.15** (US-31) every event class has an urgency: `page` for an approval request with less than `page_before` left, unattended work refused or out of budget, a stopped health controller and a failing route; `notify` for completions, failures and degraded runs; `digest` for the rest, sent in the group's declared working hours (decision 3).
- **L31.17** (US-31) every object with an expiry (an exception, a hold, a branch unit, a lend, a grant) raises `expiring` to its owning group at the platform's `expiry_warn` lead time, and `expired` when it lapses (decision 3).
- **L34.8, L34.15** (US-34) the ends of interactive processes raise `expiring` to the people attached, and drains are told at once (full rules in [ADR 0030](0030-an-interactive-process-holds-a-computer-while-someone-is-attached.md)).
