# ADR 0033: A resident artifact is held in a domain's memory and written out before it goes

**Status:** Draft · 2026-11-14
**Supersedes / relates to:** open finding L37.1 (an artifact that lives in GPU memory has no publication, import, holding, loss or write-out model); ADR 0001 and its amendments 1 and 2 (pipes are staged artifacts; non-deterministic stages; the artifact store is a device per topology, as revised by L33.13); ADR 0028 decisions 1 to 6, 8 and 9 (memory distance, domains the hardware provides, `--domain` and whole claims, a domain's healthy size, claims owned by one tree and one group, draining and scrubbing, `domain_reserve`, volatile `/dev/shm` stores); ADR 0029 decisions 2 and 7 (in place, staged or streamed; intermediate artifacts on the computer's own file system); ADR 0002 (executable annotations); ADR 0003 amendment 1 (placement); ADR 0004 amendments 1 and 2 (gang restart from checkpoint, the degraded channel and floors); ADR 0006 (receipts); ADR 0007 amendment 2 and ADR 0011 amendment 3 (class and residency); ADR 0008 (signals and preemption notices); ADR 0010 (prices); ADR 0015 (schemas); ADR 0016 decisions 2 and 7 (groups and grants); ADR 0017 decision 2 (gates from policy); ADR 0021 decision 4 (exclusion of failed components); ADR 0024 decision 8 (framework runtimes inside a process); ADR 0025 decision 9 (units); ADR 0026 (pins); the pipeline compiler §8.5; the command map rows for `cat` and `ls`

## Context

ADR 0028 lets a process tree claim a memory domain whole, an NVLink rack for instance, and keep its own data in the memory of every GPU in it. It does not let one process leave data there for another. A pipeline whose first stage cleans a table on the GPUs of a rack and whose second stage trains on it must, under ADR 0001, write the table to the artifact store and read it back: for a 6 TB table that is two passes over the store's links that the hardware could have skipped, since both stages could run in the same domain and the importer could map the exporter's memory over the fabric. Frameworks already do this inside one job (RAPIDS and Spark keep columns on the GPU, Ray keeps GPU objects in its own store), and across jobs teams do it by hand, with memory handles exported through the fabric's daemons and passed in whatever object they can find.

US-37 ran that handoff through Convoy as designed: handles in a ConfigMap that Argo CD pruned three minutes later; an importing gang placed on the other rack, where the handles meant nothing; another group importing the memory with no check; float16 expected and float32 delivered; the exporter's exit freeing the memory under 31 ranks; a sleep loop holding 24 GPUs for three days, about $14 000, to avoid writing 6 TB once; buffers updated in place mid-epoch; a lost GPU's part of the table read as zeros; a two-minute preemption notice that lost 1 TB; a `cat` from the session that hung; restricted rows left in memory as the rack changed hands; and, inside one Ray job, GPU objects Convoy could not see, freed by a reference count when a trainer restarted and silently recomputed.

Facts that constrain the design:

- Memory exported over a fabric (IMEX handles on an NVLink domain) can be imported only by processes inside the same domain; outside it the handle is meaningless and the data must be copied.
- GPU memory belongs to the process that allocated it and is freed when that process exits, however it exits; nothing in the hardware keeps it for an importer.
- GPU memory does not survive a fault that resets or excludes the GPU, and a preemption notice (two minutes on some clouds, thirty seconds on others) is shorter than the time to write terabytes to a store.
- A handle conveys an address, not a layout: the importer cannot tell float16 columns from float32 ones, or an Arrow table from a tensor, from the handle alone.
- Holding a domain's memory holds the domain's GPUs, at the price of the GPUs, whether or not they compute.

## Decision

1. **A domain's memory is a volatile tier of the artifact store.** Assuming L37.17 revises ADR 0001 amendment 2, an artifact may be `resident`: held in the memory of one domain (ADR 0028 decision 2) under the content id of its staged form, the bytes it would have in the topology's store. The planner keeps an artifact resident between stages of one process tree, or between processes of one group, placed in the same domain, when it fits the domain's healthy free memory beside the importer's own needs; otherwise it stages it as ADR 0029 decision 2 says. `explain` prints the choice, the reason and both prices. A resident artifact fits in one domain or is not resident; Convoy does not page resident artifacts to host memory or across domains (L37.18). GPU objects a framework keeps inside one process tree (collective buffers, Ray's GPU objects, Spark's cached columns) are part of that process and count in its memory bound (ADR 0028 decision 9, ADR 0024 decision 8); only artifacts published under decision 2 are resident artifacts, and what a framework shares inside its own processes is invisible to Convoy (L37.19).

2. **A resident artifact is published, registered and immutable.** A holder publishes a resident artifact through the platform toolbox, which exports the buffers through the domain's driver (IMEX on an NVLink domain) and registers the artifact in the artifact store's index with its domain, holder, layout, size, class and importers. The registration is the only place its handles live: never a Kubernetes object, a file or an environment variable in a namespace someone else manages. `ls -l /artifacts/<name>` shows `resident in <domain>`, and `/sys/compute/<id>/mem/domains/<d>/resident/` lists every resident artifact with its holder, importers, size, held-since time and cost to date. Once published, a resident artifact is immutable: importers map it read-only, the holder's own writes to published buffers are refused by the toolbox, and changed buffers are a new artifact with a new id.

3. **A resident artifact declares its layout, and an import checks it.** Publication declares the layout: the schema (ADR 0015), the data type of every column or tensor, and the memory format (Arrow columnar, a dense tensor with its strides, a framework's documented format). The importer's executable declares the layout it expects (ADR 0002). An import compares the two before any read and refuses a mismatch with exit 3, naming the first column or tensor that differs; a conversion is a stage of its own, producing a new artifact.

4. **An import is a grant, and Convoy's records keep the artifact alive.** An importer is in the holder's process tree under `--domain=@parent` (ADR 0028 decision 5), or in the holder's group with the holder's grant for that artifact (ADR 0016 decision 7); another group reads only the staged form, under the usual grants and residency. Every import is recorded in the registration with the importer's process and member. A resident artifact is kept alive by those records, not by a framework's reference counts: a member that restarts re-imports the artifact while it is resident, and a framework that drops its last reference does not free a published artifact. Any recomputation of an artifact is a step in the receipt (ADR 0006), never silent.

5. **An importer is placed in the artifact's domain, or reads a copy.** A stage that imports a resident artifact is placed only in that artifact's domain, in ADR 0025 decision 4's filter after residency and caps (ADR 0003 amendment 1). If it cannot be placed there within its `--wait`, the plan uses the staged form, written out under decision 6 at its price, or is refused with exit 3 if the contract forbids that cost. An importer in another domain of the same computer reads a priced copy over RDMA into its own memory, at distance `rdma` (ADR 0028 decision 1), never a shared mapping. A read from outside the computer, such as `cat` from a session, is a priced copy through the holder into the store or the reader's memory, or is refused with exit 3 when the price exceeds the reader's budget; it never blocks on a mapping it cannot make.

6. **A holder that ends keeps holding until its artifact is released or written out.** A holder process whose resident artifact has live importers moves to `holding` when its work exits: its code has ended and its exit status is recorded, but its claim on the domain's memory continues, charged to its group. It stays `holding` until every importer releases the artifact, until the import's declared holding period ends, or until `resident_max` passes; then the artifact is written to the topology's store, verified by content id, and only then released, and the domain drains under ADR 0028 decision 6. An import across processes declares its holding period; a period beyond the platform default `resident_hold_max` needs the approval of the holder's group (ADR 0017 decision 2), as a hold of the whole domain beyond `domain_hold_max` already does.

7. **Holding is priced and compared with staging.** Holding a resident artifact is charged to the holder's group at the price of the memory and the accelerators it keeps from other work (ADR 0010), in the computer's own unit (ADR 0025 decision 9), and shown in `ps`, in the domain's `resident/` listing and in the receipt. `explain` compares holding for the declared period with writing the artifact once and staging it for each importer; above the platform default `hold_vs_stage` it recommends staging and says by how much. Free members of a domain held only for a resident artifact count as held, not as stranded (ADR 0028 decision 8).

8. **A resident artifact that loses a part says so.** When a component that holds part of a resident artifact fails or is excluded (ADR 0021 decision 4), that part is `lost`. An importer that reads it gets `EIO`, never zeros, and the reading stage's floor applies (ADR 0004 amendment 2). Where a staged copy exists, or the receipt can recompute the part from pinned inputs, the platform rematerializes it and the receipt says so; otherwise the artifact is `lost` for that part and the importers' stages decide by their floors.

9. **A preemption notice writes out what fits.** At a preemption notice for a holder or for any member whose memory holds part of a resident artifact (ADR 0008), the platform writes to the store, in order of import, the parts that can be written within the notice; the rest is `lost` under decision 8. `explain` says beforehand, from the computer's notice period and the measured rate to the store, how much of the artifact a notice can save, and warns when a computer whose capacity can be preempted holds a resident artifact larger than that.

10. **A resident artifact carries its class, and the scrub is tied to it.** A resident artifact carries the residency and class of its inputs (ADR 0007 amendment 2, ADR 0011 amendment 3), and its domain is within that residency. A domain that holds or has held a `class=restricted` resident artifact is not offered to another group until the artifact is released and the domain's memory is cleared under ADR 0028 decision 6, and the node receipt names the artifact the scrub cleared. A resident artifact is never held past the retention of its class; at that point it is written out where the class permits storage, or released.

## Declined

| Proposal | Why not | What replaces it |
|---|---|---|
| passing memory handles in ConfigMaps, files or environment variables | pruned, unauthenticated, outside any group's control | registration in the artifact index (decision 2) |
| holding GPU memory with a sleep loop | idle accelerators at full price, with no approval and no end | `holding`, priced, with a period and an approval (decisions 6, 7) |
| mutable shared buffers between jobs | importers read half old and half new data | immutable artifacts, read-only imports (decision 2) |
| a global GPU-memory pool across domains | no fabric maps memory across domains, and copies already have a price | resident within one domain, RDMA copies beyond it (decisions 1, 5) |
| paging resident artifacts to host memory or disk | a second, hidden storage tier with its own loss and class rules | the staged form (decision 1) |
| trusting a framework's reference count | a restart frees the artifact and recomputes it silently | Convoy's import records (decision 4) |

## Consequences

- L37.1 is drafted here. US-37's other findings land on this record when US-37 is applied: L37.17 (an amendment to ADR 0001 amendment 2 that decision 1 assumes) and L37.10 (framework objects) in decision 1, L37.2 and L37.7 (registration, immutability) in decision 2, L37.15 (layout) in decision 3, L37.5 and L37.13 (importers, import records) in decision 4, L37.3, L37.9 and L37.14 (reads from outside, placement, RDMA copies) in decision 5, L37.4 and L37.11 (holding, period and approval) in decision 6, L37.6 (price) in decision 7, L37.8 (loss) in decision 8, L37.16 (preemption) in decision 9, L37.12 (class and scrub) in decision 10; L37.18 and L37.19 are accepted.
- ADR 0001 amendment 2's store gains a volatile tier in a domain's memory, with the same content ids. ADR 0028 decision 5's `--domain=@parent` carries imports within a tree, and decision 6's scrub names the artifacts it cleared. ADR 0029 decision 2's choice is unchanged; `resident` is the planner's choice between stages, not a way an input reaches its first stage. ADR 0004 amendment 2's floors apply to lost parts. ADR 0002's annotations gain the layout an executable expects.
- New paths: `/sys/compute/<id>/mem/domains/<d>/resident/` and `/sys/proc/<pid>/resident/` (what a process holds and imports). A new process state, `holding`. New platform defaults: `resident_max`, `resident_hold_max`, `hold_vs_stage`.
- ROADMAP: placement bound to the artifact's domain, imports within a claim and RDMA copies are M3; the resident tier in the artifact index, write-out and rematerialization are M4; `holding`, its approval and write-out at a preemption notice are M6; import grants, class and residency on resident artifacts and the scrub tied to them are M7; lost parts and the domain's `resident/` listing are M8; publish and import shims in the toolbox for the dataframe, tensor and actor frameworks are M9.
- Open, to be settled against this record: resident artifacts in a coherent CPU-GPU tier or in host memory shared by a domain; an inference service's weights or KV cache kept resident across requests (ADR 0028's open item); resident outputs of a stream (ADR 0032); a story that probes this record.
